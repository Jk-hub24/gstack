# Organic First

Buyers in our markets research in Reddit threads, practitioner Slack groups,
LinkedIn comments and AI answers. Ads don't reach those places well, and
technical buyers discount them on sight. Earned presence compounds. Rented
reach stops the day the card stops.

## The channel ladder

Plan from the top down. Don't move to a lower rung until the one above it has a
plan and an owner.

1. **Communities where buyers already talk.** Reddit first (posts, replies,
   long-running comparison threads), then niche forums, Slack/Discord groups,
   review site Q&A. This is also what AI engines cite, so it does double duty.
2. **Founder and expert voices.** Josh and named team members on LinkedIn.
   Comments on other people's data posts count as much as original posts.
3. **Citable content.** Articles and pages built to answer the exact prompts
   buyers type into ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude and Google. Original
   data beats opinion.
4. **Owned communities.** Topic subreddits, AMAs, run threads, newsletters.
5. **Reviews and reputation.** G2, Trustpilot, review-site answers.
6. **Partners and referrals.** Partner-recycled leads, client referrals, co-built
   research.
7. **Direct outreach.** Cold email and LinkedIn sequences. Organic in spirit:
   one person writing to another with something specific. Never blasted.
8. **Paid.** See below.

## Paid is a second step, not a starting point

The paid-advisor agent only gets involved when Josh asks for it. Even then:

- Paid amplifies a winner. It needs an organic piece or message with proof it
  works (engagement, retention, replies, citations) before a dollar goes behind
  it.
- Creative comes from the organic program. Native-looking, pain-led, written to
  the same voice rules. No corporate dashboard creative.
- Every test has a cap, an end date and a kill rule written before launch.
- Learnings flow back into organic. The point of a paid test is what it teaches
  the organic engine.
- If someone opens a plan with paid, push back and offer the organic plan first.

## What "organic" doesn't mean

- Bots, AI-posting, bought upvotes, vote rings, fake reviews. Never. They get
  purged, they get caught, and they poison every real account near them. This
  is the line that makes everything else believable.
- Brand mentions in every post. Most community posts mention nothing. The few
  that mention a product do it as one action among several, never the headline.
- Fast. Set timelines honestly: about a month of setup, citations and indexing
  from month 2 to 3, pipeline impact from around month 5. Anyone promising
  AI citations in two weeks is selling something. Say so, even when it costs
  a deal.

## How to score an organic plan

Before calling a plan done, check it has:

- Named communities, threads, prompts or accounts. Not "Reddit" but which subs
  and why.
- A content mix where most pieces give value with zero mention.
- A retention or survival metric (posts still up after 30 days), not just reach.
- A citation metric (which prompts, which engines, baseline vs now).
- A pipeline signal (replies, audit requests, demos), measured from month 3 on.
